Blockchains are unusual among data sources: everything is public, permanent and
addressable. In principle anyone can answer almost any question about what
happened on-chain. In practice almost nobody does, because the raw data is
enormous, badly shaped for human questions, and hostile to interpretation.

That gap — between data that is technically public and insight that is actually
available — is where I work.

## DataInByte

**DataInByte** is my data storytelling initiative. The tagline is *Web3 Data
Stories*, and the premise is simple: a chart is not an insight, and a metric
without a narrative around it changes nobody's mind. The work is to go from raw
on-chain data to a claim a person can understand, argue with, and act on.

## What the work involves

- **On-chain analysis** — querying raw chain data to answer a defined question
- **Cohort and retention analysis** — following groups of users over time
- **SQL** — the working language of most of it
- **Dashboards** — published on Dune, so the queries are inspectable
- **Data visualisation** — choosing the form that makes the finding legible
- **Market intelligence** — reading behaviour and flows, not just prices

## The retention question

The analysis I keep returning to is the one about attention versus retention.
Growth numbers in this industry are almost always acquisition numbers — how many
new addresses arrived. That figure is easy to produce and easy to celebrate.

The harder and more useful question is what happened to those users sixty and
ninety days later. My Solana analysis is an attempt to answer exactly that, and
the gap between the two figures is the finding.
