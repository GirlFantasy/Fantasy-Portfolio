Blockchains are unusual among data sources: everything is public, permanent, and
addressable. In principle anyone can answer almost any question about what
happened on-chain. In practice almost nobody does, because the raw data is
enormous, badly shaped for human questions, and hostile to interpretation.

That gap — between data that is technically public and insight that is actually
available — is where I work.

## DataInByte

**DataInByte** is building an on-chain adoption intelligence system for
understanding how Web3 products and ecosystems actually grow. By analysing user
behaviour, activation, retention and drop-off, I use blockchain data to move past
vanity metrics — views, followers, community size — and measure what real
adoption looks like.

Dashboards are published openly on Dune, so the queries are inspectable and the
figures reproducible rather than asserted.

## The activation question

The thread running through all of it is the difference between *arriving* and
*staying*.

Growth numbers in this industry are almost always acquisition numbers — how many
new addresses appeared. That figure is easy to produce and easy to celebrate. The
harder and more useful question is what those users did next, and whether they
were ever really activated at all.

I set out the framework in
[Activation: Where Web3 Adoption Begins](https://datainbyte.substack.com/p/activation-where-web3-adoption-begins),
and apply it across ecosystems — most extensively in the ongoing
[SEI analysis](/work/sei-user-behavior-analysis).
