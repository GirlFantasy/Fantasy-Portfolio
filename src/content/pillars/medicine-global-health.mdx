Clinical training teaches you to treat the patient in front of you. It takes
longer to see the system behind them — the reasons they arrived late, the record
that did not follow them, the resources that were never there.

That system-level view is what pulled me toward the **Sustainable Development
Goals** and **United Nations Academic Impact (UNAI)**. Health outcomes are
rarely only clinical. They sit downstream of education, equity, infrastructure
and information.

## Training

**Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery (MBBS)** — College of Medicine,
Ebonyi State University. September 2019, expected February 2027.

Alongside clinical training I have served as **Secretary Technical Officer of
NiMSA** (Nigerian Medical Students' Association) and as a **Local Officer for
Mental Health Advocacy with EMSA**.

## Millennium Fellowship

I was selected as a **Millennium Fellow** — Class of 2025, Ebonyi State
University — a leadership programme convened by the Millennium Campus Network in
partnership with UNAI, supporting student-led projects that advance the SDGs on
their own campuses.

## Her Path to Power

As **Campus Director of Her Path to Power (HPP)**, Class of 2025, at Ebonyi State
University from **September to November 2025**, I led the initiative on my
campus — work anchored in **SDG 5: Gender Equality**.

Her Path to Power is about what happens before a woman reaches a position of
influence: the confidence, the networks and the practical knowledge that decide
whether she ever gets there. Running it on a medical campus mattered to me
specifically because medicine is a field where women are well represented in
training and thin at the top.

## Research

**Knowledge, Perceptions, and Experiences of Peer-to-Peer Sexual Abuse Among
Medical Students in Abakaliki, Ebonyi State, Nigeria** — a quantitative
descriptive cross-sectional study.

Peer-to-peer sexual abuse inside medical training is under-researched, and the
conditions that make it hard to study are the same conditions that let it
persist. The study is **complete but unpublished**.

## Why this sits alongside the data work

The instinct is the same in both halves of what I do: find where a system loses
people, and measure it honestly. In global health that means asking who a
programme misses. In on-chain analysis it means asking who stopped showing up
after the first week. The subject changes; the question does not.
