## Context

Electronic health records in much of Africa are fragmented across institutions
that do not exchange data. A patient's history is effectively trapped wherever
it was created. The clinical cost is repeated tests, lost context and decisions
made on incomplete information.

Blockchain is frequently proposed as a response — a shared, tamper-evident,
patient-controlled record layer that no single institution owns. The proposal is
plausible enough to merit examination and repeated often enough to merit
scrutiny.

## The question

**What has actually been proposed, built and evaluated for blockchain-based
electronic health records in African healthcare — and what does the evidence
support?**

Distinguishing the demonstrated from the merely proposed is the point of doing
this as a systematic review rather than a narrative one.

A systematic review of the existing literature on blockchain-based electronic

## Why it matters

If the evidence supports the approach, it points toward a specific, tractable
intervention in a health system problem that affects millions of patients. If it
does not, that is equally worth establishing — implementation effort in
low-resource health systems is scarce and should not be spent on architecture
that has not demonstrated benefit.

Either result is useful. That is what makes the question worth reviewing
properly.
