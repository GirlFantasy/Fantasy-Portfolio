## Context

Peer-to-peer sexual abuse within medical training environments is
under-researched, and the conditions that make it hard to study are the same
conditions that let it persist: close-knit cohorts, long shared training years,
steep informal hierarchies, and strong disincentives to report.

Before an institution can respond to a problem, it needs to know its shape. That
requires measurement, and that is what this research aimed to address.

## The question

**What do medical students in Abakaliki know about peer-to-peer sexual abuse,
how do they perceive it, and what have they experienced?**

Separating knowledge, perception and experience matters. A population can be
well-informed and still normalise the behaviour, or recognise it clearly and
still have no viable route to report it. Those are different problems requiring
different responses.

## Method

A **quantitative descriptive cross-sectional study** was conducted among medical
students in Abakaliki, Ebonyi State, Nigeria.
