## Context

Solana's growth story is usually told with acquisition numbers. New addresses,
new wallets, new users — figures that are straightforward to produce and easy to
present as momentum.

Acquisition, on its own, tells you how many people arrived. It tells you nothing
about whether they stayed.

## The question

**Of the users a chain acquires in a given month, how many are still there two
to three months later?**

This reframes growth as a retention problem rather than an accounting one, and
it is the question that determines whether an ecosystem is compounding or
churning.

## Data & method

The analysis works from on-chain data, queried with SQL and published as
dashboards, grouping users into acquisition cohorts and following each cohort
forward.

The parameter definitions this analysis runs on — how activation is defined, and
what counts as a new and a retained user — are set out in
[Activation: Where Web3 Adoption Begins](https://datainbyte.substack.com/p/activation-where-web3-adoption-begins).

## Findings so far

Across the period analysed, Solana was acquiring on the order of **25 to 200
million new users per month**.

Of those acquired users, approximately **0.02% to 0.6% remained within the 60 to
90 day window**.

The distance between those two figures is the finding. An ecosystem can report
enormous top-line growth while retaining a fraction of a percent of the users
that growth is composed of — and the headline number gives no indication that
this is happening.

## Why it matters

Acquisition is a marketing metric. Retention is a product metric. Reporting the
first without the second describes reach, not adoption.

For anyone allocating capital, attention or engineering time against ecosystem
growth figures, the difference is not academic — it is the difference between a
market that is expanding and one that is recycling.
